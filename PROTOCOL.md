# Quantile target versus allocation in fixed-budget bandits

Written 16 November 2026 before generating the evaluation worlds. This is a local prospective record.

Implement the Q-SAR accept/reject procedure from Zhang and Ong (ICML 2021) for eight arms and two recommendations. The target is the lower 20th percentile. Separate the effect of choosing quantiles from the effect of allocating adaptively. Use the displayed descending order-statistic rank floor(n(1−τ)) for every empirical-quantile arm. Break ties by increasing randomly allocated arm ID.

Study budgets 160 and 320. Q-SAR uses the paper’s phase ceiling schedule and can spend slightly less than its budget cap. Every equal-allocation and two-stage baseline receives exactly the same total number of samples Q-SAR spends, and the same per-arm potential reward stream. Compare Q-SAR, mean-SAR, uniform quantile allocation and a two-stage quantile policy reallocating remaining samples across the four empirical boundary arms after drawing eight observations per arm. Track exact set recovery and average true quantile loss across the two selected arms. Nominal primary is Q-SAR minus uniform exact set recovery at 320-cap exponential worlds, 800 independent trials. A point gain of at least 0.02 and paired 95% lower endpoint above zero are required. Use 6,000 paired trial resamples. Compare against the two-stage baseline as a prespecified secondary stress on the interpretation.

Draw 800 fresh environments each for exponential well-separated arms, exponential near-ties, heterogeneous Weibull mixtures of hazard shapes, and two-piece mixture arms with an abrupt low-tail cutoff. Each trial randomises true rankings, per-arm outcomes and shape independently. Generate all population quantiles analytically from inverse CDFs. Match random streams between methods inside each trial. Prespecify the cutoff and keep the worst regime. The Weibull and mixture families are stress settings and need not meet the paper's theoretical assumptions. The experiment estimates simulator-conditional performance and does not evaluate aggregated feedback or an applied allocation.
